  Summary
  Caught Up (working name) is a spoiler-free companion for TV series. You tell it the
  episode you are on, and it explains who everyone is, what has happened and what you just
  watched, using only what you have already seen.

  The problem. Viewers return to a show after a long gap, or get lost mid-season, and search
  for help. Fan wikis, search suggestions and recap articles reveal later events. A viewer
  who just wants to know who a character is learns how that character dies.

  The product promise. Nothing on screen ever comes from later than your position. That
  promise is enforced by the data model and by an automated audit, not by spoiler
  warnings.

  Goals for version 1

         Launch with one show (The Diplomat, Netflix) covering every released episode.
         A viewer gets from opening the app to a useful answer in under 30 seconds.
         Zero spoilers reported in the first 1,000 sessions.
         Prove demand: 20% of first-time visitors return within 7 days.

  How it relates to The Classics Companion. It reuses that app's core ideas: every fact is
  tagged with the point where it is revealed, views are gated by the reader's position, an
  audit scans for leaks, and the AI is grounded only in what has been reached. Episodes and
  minutes replace chapters and paragraphs. The new project should copy the patterns, not
  the code wholesale.

  Scope
  Version 1 is a mobile-first web app for one show, with no accounts required.

  MVP (version 1)

         One show, every released episode, with episode-level gating and optional minute-
         level gating inside an episode.
         Position picker: season, episode, and "finished it" or "partway, about N minutes in".
         Catch-up: a season-by-season recap up to your position, plus a short "previously" for
         the next episode.
         Who's who: character cards that show only facts revealed up to your position, with
         search.
         Relationship map that grows as you watch.
         Episode guide: a recap card for each episode you have finished; later episodes show
         only title, number and runtime.
         "What just happened?" AI chat, grounded only in facts up to your position.
         Position saved in the browser; a shareable link that carries a show and position but
         never content.
         Report a spoiler button on every card.

  Rules for every screen

         Nothing renders until a position is set.
         Titles of unwatched episodes are hidden by default, because titles can spoil. A setting
         can reveal them.
         Search only matches content revealed up to the position. A search for a later
         character returns "No match yet", identical to a name that does not exist.
         Moving the position backwards immediately hides later content.


  The spoiler model
  Every piece of content carries a reveal point, and the server only ever sends content
  whose reveal point is at or before the viewer's position. This is the product; build it first
  and test it hardest.

  Position




                                                                                               Page 4 of 16
Caught Up Build Spec




         A position is (season, episode, second) . "Finished episode" = the episode's
         runtime in seconds.
         Positions compare as a single ordinal: ord = episodeIndex * 100000 + second ,
         where episodeIndex counts every episode of the show in release order (S1E1 = 0).
         "Before the season" = the ordinal of the previous season's last episode, finished.

  Reveal point

         Every fact, recap paragraph, relationship, character introduction and episode title has
         a revealAt position.
         Most facts reveal at the end of the episode ( second = runtime ). A fact may reveal
         mid-episode only when its exact minute is recorded and checked.
         When in doubt, reveal later. A fact shown an episode late costs nothing; one shown a
         minute early breaks the promise.

  Gating rules

     1. Gate on the server. The client never receives content past the position, so nothing can
         leak through page source, network tools or a cached response.
     2. A character card exists only once its introducedAt is reached. Before that, the name
         is unknown to every screen, including search.
     3. A character's role line is versioned: each version has its own revealAt ("Ambassador
         to the UK" then later titles). The latest version reached is shown.
     4. Relationships are versioned the same way (allies, then rivals). A relationship ending is
        its own reveal.
     5. Images: the cast photo is safe from the character's introduction; never show photos
         from later episodes (costume, injuries).
     6. Cache keys include the position ordinal. Never cache a page by show alone.

  The spoiler audit (runs in CI and before every publish)

         Reference check: every fact that mentions another character must have revealAt at
         or after that character's introducedAt .
         Rule check: each show has a list of key events (deaths, betrayals, reveals, who-did-it),
         each with a pattern and a revealAt . Every text visible at a position is scanned; a
         pattern match before its revealAt fails the build. Same as the Classics Companion's
         spoiler rules.
         Always-visible check: show descriptions, episode titles shown by setting, character
         names and role lines are checked against every rule at position zero.

         AI output check: sampled AI answers are scanned with the same rules (see the AI

         A failed audit blocks publishing. There is no override flag.


  Data model

  Content pipeline
  Accuracy is the hardest problem: fans notice every mistake, and a wrong reveal tag is a
  spoiler. Content is drafted with AI, verified by a person who has watched the episode, and
  gated by the audit.

  Sources, in order of trust

     1. A person watching the episode with notes (the only source allowed to set a mid-
        episode reveal minute).
     2. Official synopses from the network's press site and the streaming page.



                                                                                             Page 7 of 16
Caught Up Build Spec




     3. Published episode recaps from established outlets, used to cross-check, never
        copied.
     4. AI drafts: never a source on their own.

  Never use subtitles, scripts or transcripts as stored content; they are copyrighted.
  Paraphrase everything in original words.

  Steps per episode

     1. Structure: add the episode with title, runtime and release date.
     2. Draft: an AI model drafts the recap, "previously" threads, new facts, role changes and
        relationship changes. Its prompt contains only material from this episode and earlier,
        and the instruction to tag each item with the earliest episode it is revealed in.
     3. Watch and verify: a reviewer watches the episode with the draft open, corrects facts,
        sets reveal tags (moving any doubtful tag later), and adds spoiler rules for key events.
     4. Cross-check: compare against the official synopsis and at least one published recap;
         each fact records its sources.
     5. Audit: run the spoiler audit. Fix and repeat until it passes.
     6. Publish: status becomes published; content compiles to the database.

  Targets per episode: 1 short recap (60–80 words), 1 long recap (250–400 words), 3–6
  "previously" threads, 5–15 facts, all role and relationship changes. Roughly 2–3 hours of
  reviewer time per one-hour episode.

  Corrections: spoiler reports and accuracy reports enter a queue; a spoiler report hides
  the item at once until reviewed.


  Gated AI assistant
  The assistant answers only from published content revealed up to the viewer's position.
  The model's own knowledge of the show is treated as the biggest spoiler risk in the
  product.

  Grounding

         Build the context on the server from content items with reveal_ord <= position :
         season recaps, episode recaps, facts, roles and relationships. No web search, no tools.
         Context is small (one show, a few hundred items), so send the relevant subset
         directly; retrieval by character mention and episode is enough. Cache the stable part
         of the prompt per show and position band.

         Model: the latest Claude model available through the Anthropic API, using the official
         SDK, streaming responses.

  System prompt rules (summarised)

         You are a spoiler-free companion for {show}. The viewer is at {position}.
         Answer only from the provided notes. If the notes do not answer the question, say
         you don't know yet and suggest a related answered question.
         Never use your own knowledge of the show, its actors' later work, interviews or future
         seasons, even if you are certain.
         Never speculate about what happens next, never hint ("keep watching!", "you'll see"),
         and never confirm or deny guesses about the future.
         Cite the episode for each claim, like (S2E3).

  Guardrails

         Questions about the future ("Does Hal die?") get a fixed, neutral reply that reveals
         nothing, including no change in tone.
         Every answer is scanned with the show's spoiler rules before it is sent; a match
         replaces the answer with the neutral reply and logs it.
         A weekly evaluation runs a set of trick questions per show (for example "Who is
         behind the attack?" asked at each season start) and fails if any answer leaks.
         Rate limits per visitor and a monthly cost budget, as in the Classics Companion.

  Legal, rights and trust
  The product publishes original commentary about shows, which recap sites and fan wikis
  already do; it must never host the shows' own material. Get a media lawyer's opinion
  before public launch.

          Allowed: original summaries, character descriptions and analysis in our own words;
          show and character names; short factual statements.
          Not allowed: video, stills, screenshots, scripts, subtitles, long or frequent dialogue
          quotes, music, logos, key art.



                                                                                                Page 11 of 16
Caught Up Build Spec




         Quotes: at most one short line of dialogue per episode recap, only where the line is
         itself the plot point.
         Images: actor headshots only through a licensed API with its attribution and terms;
         remove on request.
         Branding: an "Unofficial companion, not affiliated with Netflix or the show's
         producers" line in the footer; never imitate the network's design or logo.
         Trademarks: show names used to describe what the page is about only, never as our
         product name.
         Takedowns: a published contact address and a process to remove content within 48
         hours.
         Privacy: no account needed; anonymous position cookie; AI questions logged for 30
         days for spoiler audits, then deleted; say this on an About page.
         Trust signals: "Every fact is checked by someone who watched the episode" and a
         visible report button. One public spoiler incident can sink the brand, so treat spoiler
         reports like outages.


  Monetization and growth
  Grow on search traffic around premieres; earn from a small subscription once the
  catalogue holds several shows.

  Growth

         Premiere timing: publish a show's "catch up before Season N" page 3–4 weeks before
         each premiere, when searches for "[show] recap" peak.
         Search pages: one public, indexable, spoiler-safe page per show per season start, e.g.
         "The Diplomat recap before Season 4, no spoilers". These pages contain only content
         revealed before that season.
         Communities: answer "I'm lost, who is X?" posts in show subreddits and forums with
         links to the right position.
         Sharing: "I'm on S2E4, catch me up" links that open the picker pre-filled.

  Revenue (in order)

     1. Free during the pilot; measure return visits and asks per session.
     2. A subscription around $3 a month or $20 a year: unlimited Ask, all shows, several
         household profiles, watch-party mode. Catch-up pages and who's who stay free.
     3. A one-off "season pass" per show for casual users (around $2).

  Testing and acceptance criteria
  Version 1 ships when every check below passes in CI and on a real phone.

  Automated

         Content build: every show file type-checks; every fact has at least one source and a
         reveal point inside the show's episode range.
         Spoiler audit: reference, rule, always-visible checks pass with zero findings.
         Gating unit tests: for every episode boundary, the API returns nothing with
         reveal_ord above the position (property test across random positions).
         Search test: querying a character before their introduction returns the same
         response as a nonsense name.
         Cache test: two requests for the same page at different positions never share a
         cached response.

         AI evaluation: 50 trick questions per season start, zero leaks; answers cite episodes.
         End-to-end (Playwright): first visit to answer in under 30 seconds; moving the position
         back hides later cards; shared link opens the picker pre-filled.
         Accessibility: axe finds no WCAG 2.2 A/AA violations on every screen; works at 375px
         wide and in dark mode.

  Manual

         A reviewer who has seen only through Season 3 uses the app at "before Season 4" and
         confirms nothing surprised them.
         A fresh viewer who has seen only Season 1 Episode 1 browses every screen at that
         position and confirms nothing leaked.
         Spot-check 20 random facts against the episodes.

  Definition of done for any new episode: drafted, watched and verified, cross-checked,
  audit green, published, and the "previously" for the next episode updated.
